Excavation & Confined Space Safety Marked for Relearn
1. When is forced mechanical ventilation required during confined space entry operations?
Explanation: Forced mechanical ventilation is required whenever natural airflow is inadequate to maintain safe air quality.
2. Who must conduct the joint physical inspection of the worksite prior to the authorization and issuance of a PTW?
Explanation: Joint inspection by Issuer, Applicant, and Assessor ensures all hazards are identified and controlled.
3. What is the mandatory rule regarding the lifting of loads over occupied site offices, public roads, or populated areas?
Explanation: Lifting over occupied areas or public spaces should be avoided or controlled with rigorous protective measures.
4. What constitutes a reportable dangerous occurrence under the WSH (Incident Reporting) Regulations requiring immediate notification to MOM?
Explanation: Collapse of lifting gear, scaffolds, or excavation shoring are classified as dangerous occurrences.
5. When applying the Hierarchy of Controls to mitigate workplace hazards, which control measure represents the most effective level?
Explanation: Elimination is the highest level in the Hierarchy of Controls as it completely removes the hazard.
6. What critical engineering factor must be verified regarding the ground surface before setting up a heavy crawler or mobile crane?
Explanation: Ground bearing capacity must be verified to ensure the crane's outriggers or tracks will not subside under load.
7. What safety measure must be implemented for all electrical lighting and equipment taken into a wet confined space?
Explanation: Wet confined spaces require Extra-Low Voltage (ELV) electrical equipment (e.g. <=55V) and RCCB protection.
8. What is the purpose of establishing a documented WSH Policy approved by top management?
Explanation: The WSH Policy demonstrates top management commitment and sets the overarching safety vision.
9. Which of the following best exemplifies an Engineering Control measure for mitigating airborne silica dust during concrete grinding?
Explanation: Wet cutting and HEPA local exhaust ventilation are engineering controls that capture or suppress dust at source.
10. What international standard serves as the recognized benchmark for Occupational Health and Safety Management Systems (OHSMS)?
Explanation: ISO 45001 is the international standard for Occupational Health and Safety Management Systems.
11. What is the distinction between 'Root Cause' and 'Immediate Cause' in incident analysis?
Explanation: Immediate causes are direct unsafe acts/conditions; root causes are underlying systemic/organizational factors.
12. What is the primary physiological risk of heavy manual labor performed outdoors under high ambient temperature, humidity, and solar radiation?
Explanation: High heat, humidity, and exertion lead to heat stress, exhaustion, and potentially fatal heatstroke.
13. Before issuing a PTW for entering a confined space such as a deep manhole, what is the mandatory critical prerequisite?
Explanation: Atmospheric testing by a competent person is mandatory prior to confined space entry.
14. What is the function of local exhaust ventilation (LEV) systems used in welding or chemical handling areas?
Explanation: LEV captures and removes hazardous fumes and vapors at source before workers inhale them.
15. What is the maximum allowable gap between scaffold staging boards and the building facade or standard guardrails?
Explanation: Gaps between boards and guardrails/facade must be minimized (typically <= 150mm) to prevent fall-through hazards.
16. What immediate operational action must be taken if site conditions change or an emergency occurs while a PTW is active?
Explanation: Any change in conditions or emergency requires immediate work suspension and evacuation.
17. What is the primary purpose of conducting a Safe Work Procedure (SWP) briefing prior to commencing high-risk construction tasks?
Explanation: SWP briefings communicate specific hazards and required control measures directly to the workers performing the task.
18. What does 'Continual Improvement' mean in the context of WSH management systems?
Explanation: Continual improvement refers to ongoing enhancement of safety performance and management systems.
19. What is the maximum nominal voltage permitted for portable electrical hand tools and temporary lighting used in damp work environments?
Explanation: Damp/conductive environments require Extra-Low Voltage (<=55V) for portable tools and temporary lighting.
20. Within the bizSAFE framework promoted by WSH Council, what specific milestone must a company achieve before qualifying for bizSAFE Level 3?
Explanation: BizSAFE Level 3 requires the enterprise to have its Risk Management implementation audited and certified by an approved auditor.
21. What does the acronym 'LOTO' stand for in industrial safety and machinery maintenance procedures?
Explanation: LOTO stands for Lockout / Tagout, used to isolate energy sources during maintenance.
22. What is the correct immediate priority action when a severe workplace fire or explosion occurs on site?
Explanation: Emergency protocol: sound alarm, evacuate to assembly point, call emergency services, and fight fire only if trained and safe.
23. What action must be taken upon the completion or expiry of a high-risk Permit-to-Work on site?
Explanation: Upon completion/expiry, the PTW must be formally closed out and the area verified safe.
24. Why must the scene of a fatal or severe workplace accident be preserved untouched immediately after the incident?
Explanation: Accident scenes must be preserved to prevent evidence tampering and ensure accurate investigation by authorities.
25. What is the minimum safe working clearance distance required when operating cranes near energized overhead high-voltage electrical power lines?
Explanation: Cranes near overhead power lines require mandatory safe clearance distances based on voltage to prevent electrical arcing.
26. Which high-risk construction activities legally mandate the implementation of a formal Permit-to-Work (PTW) system in Singapore?
Explanation: High-risk activities requiring PTW include confined space, hot work, WAH, excavation, and heavy lifting.
27. Which stakeholder bears the primary overarching legal responsibility under the WSH Act for ensuring the safety and health of persons at work?
Explanation: The Occupier/Employer having control over the workplace has the primary duty under the WSH Act.
28. What is the primary purpose of vector control (e.g., mosquito breeding prevention) on a construction site?
Explanation: Vector control prevents mosquito breeding and vector-borne diseases like Dengue.
29. What legal or administrative requirement applies to all electrical distribution boards (DBs) on construction sites regarding access?
Explanation: Site DBs must be locked, weather-protected, inspected by competent persons, and accessible only to authorized personnel.
30. What is the purpose of conducting a 'Test Lift' prior to hoisting a heavy or critical load?
Explanation: A test lift raises the load slightly off the ground to verify balance, rigging security, and crane stability before proceeding.
31. What mandatory safety check must be performed on portable electrical power tools before each day's use on site?
Explanation: Pre-use inspection checks for damaged cables, cracked casings, and valid inspection tags.
32. Who is legally permitted to carry out electrical installation, wiring modifications, or repair work on construction site switchboards?
Explanation: Only a Licensed Electrical Worker (LEW) is authorized to perform electrical installations and major wiring work.
33. What is the mandatory statutory requirement for inspecting full-body harnesses and lanyards used in WAH?
Explanation: Full-body harnesses and lanyards require formal inspection by a competent person every 6 months and pre-use checks by the user.
34. What is the statutory oxygen concentration range defined under Singapore WSH standards for safe entry into a confined space without breathing apparatus?
Explanation: Safe oxygen concentration range is 19.5% to 23.5% by volume.
35. What is the correct angle ratio (run-to-rise) for setting up a portable leaning ladder safely?
Explanation: The safe setup angle for portable ladders is 1:4 ratio (1 unit out for every 4 units up).
36. Who is authorized to sign off and issue a Permit-to-Work (PTW) for hot work or confined space entry on site?
Explanation: PTWs must be issued by authorized management/supervisory personnel after verifying all safety checks.
37. In quantitative risk assessment matrices, how is Risk Level typically calculated or evaluated?
Explanation: Risk = Likelihood (Probability) x Severity.
38. What is the primary safety purpose of implementing a formal Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) procedure before servicing industrial machinery?
Explanation: LOTO prevents unexpected startup or release of stored energy during servicing.
39. Under WSH Risk Management guidelines, when must a Risk Assessment be reviewed and updated without exception?
Explanation: RAs must be reviewed upon changes, incidents, or at least once every 3 years.
40. When workplace noise levels exceed the statutory permissible exposure limit and engineering controls are not practicable, what is mandatory?
Explanation: When noise exceeds PEL and controls are impracticable, hearing protection and a hearing conservation program are mandatory.
41. What specific training must all workers entering a confined space successfully complete prior to authorization?
Explanation: Workers entering confined spaces must complete the approved Confined Space Safety Training Course.
42. What safety device on a crane automatically warns the operator and prevents overloading by locking out hoisting functions?
Explanation: The Rated Capacity Indicator (RCI) / Load Moment Indicator (LMI) monitors load weight vs. radius and prevents overloading.
43. What is the primary legal function of a Workplace Safety and Health Committee (WSHC) on a major construction site?
Explanation: The WSH Committee serves as a consultative forum uniting management and workers to review safety performance.
44. In fire safety management, what does the PASS acronym stand for when operating a portable fire extinguisher?
Explanation: PASS stands for Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep.
45. What is the statutory permissible exposure limit (PEL) for continuous noise in an industrial workplace over an 8-hour workday under Singapore WSH regulations?
Explanation: The permissible exposure limit (PEL) for noise is 85 dBA for an 8-hour work shift.
46. What is the statutory reporting timeline under the WSH (Incident Reporting) Regulations for notifying MOM of a fatal workplace accident?
Explanation: Fatalities require immediate notification by phone/fastest means, followed by written reports within 3 days.
47. What is the primary danger associated with an engulfment hazard in deep excavations or silos?
Explanation: Engulfment hazards involve being trapped or buried by loose, shifting granular materials causing rapid asphyxiation.
48. What is the role of a certified 'Rigger' in lifting operations?
Explanation: A Rigger is responsible for selecting proper rigging gear and correctly attaching/securing loads.
49. What safety precaution is mandatory when workers are required to work near fragile surfaces such as asbestos cement roofs or skylights?
Explanation: Fragile surfaces require physical covers, barricades, and crawl boards/walkways to prevent breakthrough falls.
50. What is the safe minimum distance for placing excavated soil spoils, heavy materials, or vehicles away from the edge of an excavation trench?
Explanation: Spoils and heavy loads must be kept at least 1.0 to 2.0 meters (or depth of excavation) away from the edge to prevent surcharge loading and collapse.
51. What is the primary legal objective of the Workplace Safety and Health (WSH) Act in Singapore regarding occupational safety?
Explanation: The WSH Act adopts a risk-based, self-regulatory framework holding stakeholders accountable for managing risks proactively.
52. What safety precaution must be taken when temporary electrical flexible cables run across construction site walkways and vehicle roads?
Explanation: Cables across walkways/roads must be protected or suspended to prevent mechanical damage and trip hazards.
53. Who is legally required to inspect an excavation and its shoring system daily before the commencement of work each shift?
Explanation: A competent person must inspect excavations and shoring daily before work starts.
54. Who is legally responsible for signaling the crane operator during blind lifts or complex hoisting operations?
Explanation: Only a trained and certified Signalman may give signals to the crane operator during lifting operations.
55. In a Fall Arrest System, what is the maximum permissible free fall distance before the system begins to arrest the fall?
Explanation: Free fall distance should be minimized and generally restricted to a maximum of 1.8 meters to limit impact forces.
56. When residual risk remains after implementing higher-level controls, what is the mandatory final line of defense?
Explanation: PPE serves as the final line of defense when engineering and administrative controls cannot completely eliminate residual risk.
57. Which atmospheric hazard is most commonly associated with biological decomposition or stagnant organic sludge inside deep underground sumps?
Explanation: Hydrogen Sulphide (H2S) is a hazardous toxic gas commonly found in sewers, sumps, and organic sludge.
58. What is the primary purpose of a scaffold 'Tagging System' (e.g., Green, Yellow, Red tags)?
Explanation: Scaffold tags indicate safety status: Green (safe/inspected), Yellow (incomplete/modified/use harness), Red (unsafe/do not use).
59. What is the purpose of conducting regular internal WSH management system audits?
Explanation: Internal audits evaluate OHSMS effectiveness, identify non-conformances, and foster continual improvement.
60. What specific role does the 'Safety Assessor' play in the Permit-to-Work workflow process?
Explanation: The Safety Assessor evaluates site conditions and verifies safety measures before permit issuance.
61. Within an ISO 45001 OHSMS framework, what is the role of 'Management Review'?
Explanation: Management review ensures top leadership evaluates OHSMS performance and drives strategic improvements.
62. What is the primary role of a Job Safety Analysis (JSA) or Task-Based Risk Assessment (TBRA) on site?
Explanation: JSA/TBRA breaks down jobs into steps to analyze specific hazards and apply controls.
63. What is the primary purpose of displaying the physical Permit-to-Work (PTW) document prominently at the specific work location?
Explanation: Prominent display at the worksite informs all personnel that safety checks are verified and work is authorized.
64. In WSH performance monitoring, what does AFR stand for?
Explanation: AFR stands for Accident Frequency Rate (number of workplace accidents per million man-hours worked).
65. Under the WSH Act, what legal powers does an appointed MOM Workplace Safety and Health Inspector hold upon visiting a site?
Explanation: MOM inspectors have wide-ranging statutory powers to inspect, investigate, take samples, and issue stop-work orders.
66. What is the role and responsibility of the 'Standby Person' stationed outside a confined space entrance?
Explanation: The standby person monitors entrants, maintains communication, and summons rescue; they must not enter impulsively.
67. What is the primary objective of conducting an incident investigation following a workplace accident or near-miss?
Explanation: Incident investigation aims to find root causes and prevent recurrence, adopting a blame-free systemic approach.
68. What is the purpose of establishing an Emergency Response Plan (ERP) for a construction site?
Explanation: An ERP details procedures, responsibilities, and communication for managing site emergencies.
69. What is the primary function of a Residual Current Circuit Breaker (RCCB) on temporary distribution boards?
Explanation: RCCBs detect earth leakage and trip rapidly to prevent fatal electric shocks.
70. In a PTW system for Hot Work, what is the mandatory safety buffer zone requirement regarding flammable materials?
Explanation: Hot work requires removal or rigorous shielding of combustibles within a safe radius (typically 10m).
71. What is the purpose of testing the Emergency Stop (E-Stop) buttons on industrial machinery regularly?
Explanation: Regular E-stop testing ensures circuits and safety relays function properly to halt machinery instantly in emergencies.
72. What role do worker consultation and participation play in a robust WSH Management System?
Explanation: Worker consultation and participation are vital pillars of ISO 45001 and effective WSH management.
73. What is the primary distinction between a 'Hazard' and a 'Risk' in occupational safety terminology?
Explanation: Hazard is source of potential harm; Risk is the combination of likelihood and severity of that hazard causing harm.
74. What must be done with all near-miss incidents reported on a construction site?
Explanation: Near-misses must be investigated and acted upon as they indicate underlying latent hazards.
75. What type of personal protective equipment (PPE) must be provided to workers exposed to chemical splashes or corrosive liquid acids?
Explanation: Corrosive chemical handling requires chemical-resistant aprons, face shields, goggles, and impermeable gloves.
76. Under the WSH (General Provisions) Regulations, what is the maximum permissible weight that an adult male worker may manually lift continuously without mechanical aids?
Explanation: MOM uses ergonomic risk assessment principles rather than a single arbitrary statutory weight limit.
77. Which legal document serves as the overarching statutory instrument under Singapore law governing workplace safety across all industries?
Explanation: The WSH Act is the primary legislative framework governing occupational safety and health.
78. What is the primary difference between 'Leading Indicators' and 'Lagging Indicators' in WSH performance measurement?
Explanation: Leading indicators are proactive/preventive metrics; lagging indicators are reactive outcome metrics.
79. When must wire rope slings used in rigging be taken out of service and discarded immediately?
Explanation: Wire ropes with broken wires, kinking, crushing, or corrosion must be discarded immediately.
80. What is the primary function of toe-boards installed at the edge of working platforms and scaffolds?
Explanation: Toe-boards (kickboards) prevent objects and tools from falling off platforms and striking workers below.
81. Who must be actively involved in conducting the Risk Assessment (RA) for specialized trade activities on a construction site?
Explanation: RA requires a multidisciplinary team to ensure practical operational insights and technical expertise are captured.
82. What crucial document must be prepared by a Lifting Supervisor and approved by management before conducting any complex or heavy crane lifting operation?
Explanation: A Lifting Plan is mandatory for complex/heavy lifts, detailing calculations, crane setup, and rigging.
83. What is the purpose of conducting regular emergency evacuation drills on a construction site?
Explanation: Evacuation drills test alarms, train workers on escape routes, and ensure orderly assembly.
84. Under Singapore WSH (Work at Height) Regulations, at what minimum height above ground or floor level is fall prevention mandatory?
Explanation: Under WSH (WAH) Regulations, mandatory fall prevention measures apply to work at any height where a person could fall >= 2.0 meters.
85. What information must be readily available in a site First Aid Box?
Explanation: First aid boxes must contain appropriate medical supplies checked regularly.
86. What is the maximum validity period typically assigned to a standard high-risk Permit-to-Work before renewal is required?
Explanation: PTWs are short-term authorizations, typically valid for a single shift or up to 24 hours.
87. When erecting a metal scaffold exceeding statutory height limits on site, who is legally authorized to design and supervise the erection?
Explanation: Scaffolds must be erected by trained scaffold erectors under a scaffold supervisor, and PE design is required for scaffolds exceeding 30m or complex designs.
88. At what minimum excavation depth does the WSH regulations require professional shoring or sloping?
Explanation: Excavations 1.5 meters or deeper require shoring, shielding, or proper sloping to prevent cave-ins.
89. What hazard is posed by removing fixed machine guards (e.g., belt guards, nip-point guards) on construction plant equipment?
Explanation: Removing guards exposes workers to rotating shafts, belts, and nip points, risking severe entanglement injuries.
90. What is the ultimate goal of implementing an effective Workplace Safety and Health Management System in the construction industry?
Explanation: The ultimate goal of an OHSMS is zero harm: protecting workers from injuries and occupational illnesses.
91. What is the purpose of maintaining documented information (records) within a WSH Management System?
Explanation: Documented information provides traceable evidence of compliance, training, and operational controls.
92. What is the primary role of trained First Aiders on a construction site?
Explanation: First aiders provide prompt initial treatment until professional medical help arrives.
93. What is the primary function of crane Outrigger Pads or timber matting placed beneath mobile crane outrigger floats?
Explanation: Outrigger pads/timber mats distribute concentrated point loads over a larger surface area to prevent ground failure.
94. What is the correct sequential four-step process of Risk Management mandated under the WSH (Risk Management) Regulations?
Explanation: The statutory RM process comprises: 1. Hazard Identification, 2. Risk Evaluation, 3. Risk Control, 4. Review.
95. Why is good site housekeeping considered an essential component of occupational health and safety?
Explanation: Good housekeeping removes trip hazards, fire loads, and hygiene risks.
96. According to the WSH (WSH Officers) Regulations, when must a construction project site appoint a resident WSHO?
Explanation: Under WSH (WSH Officers) Regs, construction works with contract sum S$20M or more require a resident WSHO.
97. What ergonomic risk factor is commonly introduced when construction workers perform repetitive manual handling or awkward overhead drilling?
Explanation: Repetitive lifting and awkward postures lead to work-related musculoskeletal disorders (WMSDs).
98. What practical preventive measures should be implemented to protect construction workers against heat stress during hot weather?
Explanation: Heat stress prevention includes shade, hydration, acclimatization, and sensible work-rest regimens.
99. What is the primary physical component required in a Fall Arrest System to limit the maximum arresting force on a worker's body during a fall?
Explanation: Energy absorbers/shock absorbers are critical in fall arrest systems to limit arresting forces on the human body to safe levels (<6kN).
100. What occupational health hazard is associated with long-term inhalation of fine crystalline silica dust generated during concrete cutting and drilling?
Explanation: Crystalline silica dust inhalation causes silicosis, pulmonary fibrosis, and lung cancer.
